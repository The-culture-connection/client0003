# Mortar Beta — Plain-Language Guide for the Team

**For:** the Mortar program team (non-technical)
**Companion to:** the full technical runbook (`BETA_TESTING_PLAN.md`) — you don't need to read that one.
**Date:** 2026-06-17

---

## Part 1 — The context (why we're doing this)

Mortar has two products that are supposed to work together like two halves of one experience:

- **The Digital Curriculum** — our online courses. People watch lessons, take quizzes, and earn certificates that teach them how to run a successful business.
- **The Expansion Network** — our alumni app. People connect with other alumni, message each other, join groups, post and find jobs/skills, attend events, and get matched with people who can help their business.

**The big idea we're testing:** someone learns business skills in the Curriculum → joins the Expansion Network as an alum → meets other alumni who help them (and whom they help back) → that activity keeps them coming back and spending money with Mortar → which keeps Mortar growing. It's a circle that should feed itself.

A "beta" is just a **supervised trial run** with a small group of real people **before** we open the doors to everyone. We do it to catch problems early and to find out whether the big idea actually works in real life — not just on paper.

**The four things we most want to find out, in order of importance:**

1. **Do alumni actually help each other?** (Our #1 question.) When we introduce two alumni, does anything real come of it — a job lead, a skill traded, an introduction, a partnership?
2. **Do people use both apps, not just one?** Does learning in the Curriculum pull them into the Network, and vice versa?
3. **Does the curriculum actually teach them something they use?** Do people know more — and *do* something differently in their business — after taking it?
4. **Will people pay?** For a course, an event ticket, or merchandise.

## Part 2 — The instructions (what we do, week by week)

The whole beta runs **6 weeks**. Think of it in three stages.

### Stage 1 — Weeks 1 & 2: "We test it ourselves"

Our own staff (about 5–8 of us) use both apps first and try to break things, so real users never hit an obvious problem.

**What the team does:**
1. Each staff member is assigned a "role" to play (a brand-new student, an alum, an admin) and one or both apps.
2. Go through everything a real user would: sign up, take a lesson, earn a certificate, send a message, post a job, register for an event, buy something.
3. Whenever something breaks, looks wrong, or is confusing — **write it down** (see "How to report a problem" below).

**We don't move on until:** nothing is seriously broken, and we've tried every major activity at least once.

### Stage 2 — Week 3: "We fix what we found"

The tech team fixes the problems from Stage 1. The program team uses this week to **line up the outside testers** and write the invitation emails.

**What the program team does this week:**
1. Build the list of outside testers (details in the next section).
2. Draft the welcome/invite email and have it ready to send.
3. Get the sign-off that everything's fixed and we're "ready for real people."

**We don't move on until:** the serious problems are fixed and the invitation has been tested on one practice account from start to finish.

### Stage 3 — Weeks 4, 5 & 6: "Real people try it"

Now we invite our outside testers (about 30 people — see below) and watch how it goes.

- **Week 4 — Get them in and learning.** Send invitations. Make sure everyone successfully signs up in both apps. Have them start a course. **Ask them a few quick questions up front** about their business knowledge (this is our "before" snapshot — we can't get it later, so don't skip it).
- **Week 5 — Connecting and buying.** Encourage alumni to look at their suggested matches, message each other, set up a call, and post/browse jobs and skills. Open up the things people can pay for (a course, an event ticket, merch) and see if anyone buys. Run at least one real event.
- **Week 6 — Wrap up and decide.** Ask the same business-knowledge questions again (the "after" snapshot). Interview a handful of alumni about whether any connection actually helped them. Look at all the numbers and stories together and decide: **are we ready to launch?**

**Throughout Weeks 4–6 (the rhythm):**
- Every day: someone checks for any serious problem reports.
- Twice a week: a short 30-minute check-in call with testers to hear how it's going and nudge anyone who's gone quiet.
- Once a week: pull the numbers so we're not scrambling at the end.

### Who the outside testers are (about 30 people)

| Group | How many | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| **Alumni** | About **20** (at least **12**) | They're the heart of the test — they're the ones who connect, message, and help each other. If we can't get at least 12, we should **wait**, because the network needs enough people to be useful. |
| **Students** | About **10** | They test the learning, certificates, and buying experience. (Students don't get the alumni app — that's by design.) |

**The most important recruiting tip:** hand-pick alumni who **already run a business** and who **need different things from each other** — for example, someone who needs a graphic designer *and* someone who offers design. The whole point is for them to help each other, so we need real "I can help you / you can help me" pairs in the group. This one choice matters more than almost anything else.

### How to report a problem (everyone uses the same method)

When something goes wrong or feels off, write it down with:
- **What you were doing** (e.g., "trying to buy the course"),
- **What happened** vs. what you expected,
- **How bad it is**, using these labels:

| Label | Means | Example |
|-------|-------|---------|
| **Blocker** | Can't continue / lost money / a real problem | App crashes; payment fails; can't sign up |
| **Major** | Important thing is broken | Messaging doesn't send |
| **Minor** | Annoying but you can work around it | A button is in the wrong place |
| **Polish** | Cosmetic | A typo, odd spacing |

Send it to the beta Slack channel or the feedback form. The tech team handles fixes; "Blocker" and "Major" issues get looked at the same day.

## Part 3 — Reading the results (how we'll know it worked)

At the end we look at **two scorecards**. We launch only if **both** look good.

### Scorecard A — "Does it work?" (the basics)
- The apps don't crash for people.
- Nobody is stuck on a "Blocker" problem.
- The key things work for real: signing up, learning, getting reminders, paying, and emails arriving.

If this scorecard fails, we're not ready — full stop.

### Scorecard B — "Does the big idea work?" (our four questions)

Because this is a small group, we pair every **number** with **real stories** from people. A number alone doesn't count — we want to hear *why*.

| Our question | What "good" looks like | The story we need |
|--------------|------------------------|-------------------|
| **1. Do alumni help each other?** | At least **5** real cases where a connection led to something concrete (a lead, a skill traded, an intro, a partnership) | 5 alumni telling us, in their words, how a connection helped their business |
| **2. Do people use both apps?** | At least **half** the alumni active in both the Curriculum and the Network | Testers describing the two as one connected experience, not two separate things |
| **3. Does the curriculum teach?** | A clear "before vs. after" improvement for most people who finish a module, plus **5** people who *did something* in their business because of it | 5 "I changed X in my business because of a lesson" stories |
| **4. Will people pay?** | At least **5** real purchases, across at least **2** of: courses, event tickets, merch | Buyers explaining why it was worth it; non-buyers explaining what stopped them |

*(These targets are starting points for a ~30-person group — we can adjust them with the tech lead once we see the real cohort.)*

### Making the call

- **Both scorecards good →** launch, and keep improving.
- **Scorecard A fails →** not ready; fix and re-test.
- **Scorecard A good but Scorecard B is weak →** the apps work, but the *idea* needs more proof. We decide together: launch and keep refining, or extend the trial to strengthen the weak spot. Either way, we write down the decision and why.
